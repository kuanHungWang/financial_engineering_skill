# Model documentation and validation report: <trade id>

Default outline, structured after the Federal Reserve / OCC Supervisory Guidance on Model Risk Management (SR 11-7):
model development, implementation and use, and model validation (conceptual soundness, ongoing monitoring, outcomes
analysis). Governance, policies and controls are out of scope; they are set by each organisation's own framework.
Each section says what it should contain; the bracketed names are sections of materials.md to draw on.

Start with a short identification table [Trade and run] and the sentence on scope above. If the market data is mock,
say so prominently at the top.

## 1. Executive summary
What was priced; the price with its confidence interval, method and model [Key results]; how many tests passed, and
every WARN / FAIL in one line each [Test summary]; the main limitations and the conclusion, in a few sentences.

## 2. Model purpose and intended use
Business purpose (valuation, MTM, independent price verification, risk), users, the product scope the model
covers, and uses it is not intended for.

## 3. Product description
The term sheet in plain words: underlyings, dates, coupons, barriers and their observation, early termination,
who holds which right. Then how each feature maps to an engine component [Components as specified].

## 4. Methodology and conceptual soundness
Model dynamics and why they fit this product's risk drivers; the numerical method and why [Engine method];
calibration and its fit [Calibration], other diagnostics [Model diagnostics]; alternatives considered (refer to
section 11).

## 5. Data and inputs
Source and date of each market input, the fixings and their source, transformations applied, data quality checks,
and whether any data is mock [Market data, Input files].

## 6. Assumptions and limitations
The engine-derived limitations [Engine-derived limitations] plus product-specific ones; how each could bias the
price (direction, rough size) and any compensating measure. Warnings of the pricing run [Warnings and notes].

## 7. Implementation and process verification
The checks and their outcome [Implementation and process verification]; explain any WARN / FAIL.

## 8. Numerical convergence and stability
Convergence in paths and dispersion across seeds [Numerical convergence and stability]; whether the path count is
sufficient for the purpose.

## 9. Sensitivity analysis
The sensitivities [Sensitivities] and whether their signs and sizes make sense for the product; which are not
significant against their standard error; sticky-moneyness or sticky-strike for spot bumps.

## 10. Stress testing
The scenarios [Stress scenarios]: where the product loses most and why.

## 11. Benchmarking
Closed-form cross-check and benchmark models [Benchmarking]; explain the gaps.

## 12. Outcomes analysis
Comparison with outside prices [Outcomes analysis]; state plainly if none were supplied.

## 13. Limiting and boundary cases
The cases, their rationale and outcome [Limiting and boundary cases].

## 14. Ongoing monitoring
What to rerun and when, which results trigger a review, market conditions that stress the model's assumptions.

## 15. Findings and conclusion
Findings (id, description, impact, recommendation), or "none". Whether the model is fit for the stated purpose
and under which conditions. No approval, rating or sign-off.

## Appendices
A. Trade specification as priced [Trade specification as priced]. B. Expected cashflows [Expected cashflows].
C. Environment and reproduction [Environment and reproduction].
